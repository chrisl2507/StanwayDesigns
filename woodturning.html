Small Batch. Handcrafted. Lasting.

The lathe is where it begins.

Woodturning is the most direct conversation between maker and material. A piece of timber goes onto the lathe raw - with its own grain, figure, and character - and comes off as something useful. A bowl that holds fruit on a kitchen table. A vessel that sits on a shelf and catches light differently every morning.

I work in small batches, selecting timber carefully and turning each piece by hand. No two are identical. The natural variation in grain and colour is what makes them worth having - not a flaw to be designed out, but the whole point.

Pieces are available ready-made through the shop, or commissioned to a specific brief. If you have a timber in mind, a size, a purpose - get in touch and I'll make it.

Materials

I work primarily in English hardwoods - walnut, oak, cherry, yew, and burr elm - sourced locally where possible. Some pieces use more exotic timbers: African Padauk, Camphor Laurel, or spalted beech, chosen for their particular colour or figure. Every blank is selected by hand before it goes on the lathe.

"Each piece is unique. Grain, figure, and natural edge vary - that's the point."

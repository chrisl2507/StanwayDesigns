About

About Me

Furniture maker, Nottingham

"The craft runs in the family"

I come from a family of furniture people. My dad and uncle both spent years in office furniture design before moving into consulting, so I grew up with timber and joinery as background noise. It got under my skin early — I just loved making things with my hands, and honestly, I never really stopped.

I went and did a BA in Furniture and Design, which was brilliant. Not because anyone told me to, but because I wanted to properly understand the craft — the techniques people have been perfecting for centuries. There's something about learning the right way to do things that makes you fall in love with it all over again.

One of the first pieces that really meant something to me was a ring box I made for my sister's wedding. Walnut and brass, hand-fitted, with a lining I must've changed three times until it felt right. It's small, it's personal, and it reminded me that the best work comes from caring about the person it's for — not just the timber.

My proudest build so far is The Odyssey — a storage and display piece that absolutely pushed me to my limit. Every joint, every detail, every finish. I could talk about that build for hours (and I will, if you let me).

I won't send anything out of my workshop that I wouldn't want in my own home. That's the rule. Most of my work comes through word of mouth from people around Nottingham, and that suits me perfectly — I'd rather do fewer things brilliantly than rush through a dozen.

The workshop

Where it happens

I built my workshop at home from scratch — it's not flashy, but everything's exactly where I need it. Good light, decent space, and no distractions. This is where the actual making happens: rough timber gets shaped, joints get fitted, and finishes get applied by hand until they're spot on. It's my favourite place to be.

Timber

The timber I use

I pick timber to suit the piece, not the other way around. Every species has its own character, grain, and personality — and honestly, choosing the right one is one of my favourite parts of any project.

African Padauk

Vivid reddish-orange tones that deepen to a rich burgundy over time. Exceptionally hard and durable, ideal for statement dining tables and decorative pieces that demand attention.

White Oak

Strong, water-resistant, and beautifully figured. A warm golden hue with pronounced grain that suits both contemporary and traditional designs. My most versatile timber.

Beech

Pale, clean, and consistent. Fine grain with a smooth finish that takes stains beautifully. Ideal for kitchen cabinetry, children's furniture, and pieces requiring a refined, understated look.

English Yew

Rich amber and brown tones with dramatic swirling grain. One of Britain's most characterful native timbers. Each piece is unique — perfect for bespoke commissions and sculptural work.

Cherry

Warm reddish-brown tones that mature gracefully with exposure to light. Fine, straight grain with a natural lustre. Suited to elegant furniture, handcrafted pieces, and bedroom commissions.

I'm fussy about where my timber comes from — quality and character always come first. Every piece gets chosen for its grain, colour, and how it's going to work in the finished build.

Some of my favourite projects have started with clients bringing their own materials — reclaimed beams from a family home, timber with a story already attached. I love that. There's something special about working with wood that's already been part of someone's life.

Location

Based in Nottingham

I'm based in Nottingham and work with clients across Nottinghamshire and the surrounding areas. Every commission includes delivery and installation — I'll make sure your piece is set up exactly where it needs to be.

If you're further afield, drop me a message anyway. I'm always happy to have a chat about what you're after.

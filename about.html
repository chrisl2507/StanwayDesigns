About

The Craftsman

Where heritage meets modern craftsmanship

"Woodworking runs in the family"

My father and uncle both began their careers specialising in office furniture design before moving into consulting. I grew up watching them sketch, problem-solve, and build. The language of timber and joinery was spoken at the dinner table.

I completed a BA in Furniture and Design, not because I needed permission to build, but because I wanted to understand the depth of the craft. The history. The techniques that have been refined over centuries. The discipline required to make something that lasts.

One of the first commissions I truly cared about was a ring box for my sister's wedding. Something small, deeply personal, and entirely hers. Walnut and brass, hand-fitted, with a lining chosen for the way it felt. That piece reminded me why this work matters — not scale or complexity, but the quiet permanence of something made with care.

The Workshop

Where it happens

Purpose-built. Every tool has its place. The light is good. The space is designed for focus, not show. This is where raw timber becomes furniture. Where sketches are tested, joints are fitted, and finishes are hand-applied until they are exactly right.

Our Materials

The timber we choose

Each wood species brings its own character, grain pattern, and story. We select every piece for beauty and longevity.

Walnut

Dark, rich, warm. A beautiful depth of colour and tight grain. Ages gracefully. Our most requested timber for dining tables and statement pieces.

Oak

Strong, durable, characterful. Pronounced grain and excellent structural properties. Works well for larger pieces requiring robustness.

Ash

Light, elegant, refined. Subtle grain and excellent workability. Suits contemporary designs and lighter spaces.

Reclaimed

Every piece tells a story. Character, history, unique markings that cannot be replicated. Perfect for clients who want something one-of-a-kind.

We source timber locally wherever possible. The specifics matter less than the quality. Each piece is selected for character, not just consistency.

Sometimes clients bring their own materials — reclaimed beams from a family property, timber with a story already attached. We welcome that. The best commissions often start with a conversation about what the wood has already lived through.

About

About Me

Furniture maker, Nottingham

"The craft runs in the family"

My father and uncle both started their careers specialising in office furniture design before going into consulting. I grew up surrounded by it — timber and joinery were the language at the dinner table. I found my passion for working with my hands early, and it stuck.

I did a BA in Furniture and Design. Not because I needed permission to build, but because I wanted to understand the depth of the craft. The techniques that have been refined over centuries. The discipline it takes to make something that lasts.

One of the first commissions that really mattered to me was a ring box for my sister's wedding. Small, personal, and made with the care you'd give something that marks a moment in someone's life. Walnut and brass, hand-fitted, with a lining chosen for the way it felt. That piece reminded me why I do this — not for scale or complexity, but for the quiet permanence of something crafted with care.

My proudest piece to date is The Odyssey — a storage and display piece that pushed my design and my craft to their limit. The detail in that build is something I'm genuinely proud of.

I don't compromise on quality. Full stop. Every piece that leaves my workshop has to be something I'd put in my own home. Most of my work comes through word of mouth from people in the Nottingham area, and I'd like to keep it that way.

The workshop

Where it happens

I converted a space at home into a purpose-built workshop. Everything has a place and a purpose. The light is good, the layout is designed for focus, not show. This is where raw timber becomes furniture — where sketches are tested, joints are fitted, and finishes are hand-applied until they're exactly right.

Timber

The timber I use

I choose the timber to suit the piece, not the other way around. Each species brings its own character, grain, and story. Most of what I use is locally sourced.

African Padauk

Vivid reddish-orange tones that deepen to a rich burgundy over time. Exceptionally hard and durable, ideal for statement dining tables and decorative pieces that demand attention.

White Oak

Strong, water-resistant, and beautifully figured. A warm golden hue with pronounced grain that suits both contemporary and traditional designs. My most versatile timber.

Beech

Pale, clean, and consistent. Fine grain with a smooth finish that takes stains beautifully. Ideal for kitchen cabinetry, children's furniture, and pieces requiring a refined, understated look.

English Yew

Rich amber and brown tones with dramatic swirling grain. One of Britain's most characterful native timbers. Each piece is unique — perfect for bespoke commissions and sculptural work.

Cherry

Warm reddish-brown tones that mature gracefully with exposure to light. Fine, straight grain with a natural lustre. Suited to elegant furniture, small goods, and bedroom pieces.

I source timber carefully, prioritising quality and character over convenience. Each piece is selected for its grain, colour, and structural integrity.

Sometimes clients bring their own materials — reclaimed beams from a family property, timber with a story already attached. I welcome that. The best commissions often start with a conversation about what the timber has already lived through.

Location

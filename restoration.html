Furniture Worth Keeping

Some pieces deserve a second life.

Old furniture was often made better than new furniture. Solid timber, hand-cut joints, finishing techniques that took days rather than minutes. When those pieces are damaged, worn, or broken — the right response isn't replacement. It's restoration.

I work on antique and period furniture across the full range of restoration disciplines: structural repair, surface stripping and refinishing, French polishing, and sympathetic reproduction work where a component has been lost or damaged beyond saving. The aim is always to return a piece to use without erasing its history.

Reproduction work is undertaken where the original cannot be salvaged — a replacement rail turned to match the existing profile, a carved detail recut in the same timber. The piece stays whole. The craftsmanship is honoured.

What I offer

Restoration services

Each piece is assessed individually. Get in touch with photos and a description and I'll advise on what's possible.

  • 01 Structural Repair Joints, frames, broken components Re-gluing failed joints, replacing broken stretchers or legs, consolidating loose frames — the work that makes a piece safe to use again.
  • 02 Surface Restoration Stripping, refinishing, French polish Careful stripping of failing finishes, surface preparation, and refinishing in period-appropriate materials — including traditional shellac-based French polish.
  • 03 Upholstery Referral We work with trusted partners Upholstery isn't my trade, but I work alongside trusted craftspeople who do it properly. I'll coordinate where a piece needs both.
  • 04 Reproduction Work Sympathetic matching for missing components New components made to match the original — same timber species, same profiles, same construction methods. Invisible when done well.
  • 05 Antique & Period Furniture Sensitive restoration that respects provenance Georgian, Victorian, Arts & Crafts, mid-century. Approached with the care and restraint that pieces of age and character deserve.
A note on honesty

We don't restore everything. If a piece isn't worth saving structurally, we'll tell you. And if it is, we'll treat it with the care it deserves.

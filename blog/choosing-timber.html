How to Choose Timber for Your Dining Table

The timber you choose for a dining table determines everything — how it feels to sit at, how it ages, how it holds up to daily use. It is the single most important decision in the commission process, and it deserves proper thought.

I work with five primary species, each with distinct character. Here is what you should know about each before making your choice.

African Padauk

If you want presence, Padauk delivers. It arrives in vivid reddish-orange tones that gradually deepen to a rich burgundy over months and years. It is exceptionally hard — harder than most European timbers — which makes it ideal for a table that will see heavy daily use. The grain is tight and interlocking, giving it a natural resistance to warping. Choose Padauk if you want a table that commands the room and rewards patience as its colour matures.

White Oak

My most versatile timber. White Oak has a warm, golden tone with pronounced grain that gives each piece visual depth without being overpowering. It is naturally water-resistant, which matters for a surface where glasses are set down and spills happen. Structurally, it is excellent — strong enough for large table tops without excessive thickness. If you are unsure where to start, White Oak is a safe and beautiful choice.

Beech

Beech is the quiet option. Pale, consistent, and clean. Its fine grain takes stains and finishes beautifully, so if you have a specific colour in mind, Beech gives you the most control. It works well in lighter, more contemporary spaces where you want the table to complement rather than dominate. It is also excellent for families with young children — its smooth, forgiving surface handles daily life well.

English Yew

Yew is for clients who want something no one else has. The grain is dramatic — swirling ambers, browns, and occasional flashes of orange. No two boards are alike. It is one of Britain's most characterful native timbers, and it carries a sense of history that newer species cannot replicate. Yew dining tables are statement pieces. They start conversations. If that appeals to you, nothing else compares.

Cherry

Cherry starts lighter than you expect and deepens steadily into a warm, reddish-brown over time. This natural ageing process is one of its greatest qualities — the table you sit at in year five will look noticeably different from the one you sat at in year one, and it will be more beautiful for it. Cherry has a fine, straight grain with a natural lustre that needs minimal finishing. It suits elegant, understated interiors.

The right timber depends on your space, your lifestyle, and how you want the table to feel. I'm happy to discuss the options in detail during a consultation — bring photos of your room and I'll guide you from there.
